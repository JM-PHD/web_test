Look Up! A real-time demonstration of orbital crowding

Space systems play an important role in addressing climate change and the UN Sustainable Development Goals, yet the rapid growth in the number of satellites is raising serious questions about the sustainability of the space industry. With more than 12,000 active spacecraft in orbit, the impact on the night sky is increasingly visible, affecting both communities and scientific astronomy through bright streaks and interference with telescope observations.

Researchers at the University of Manchester developed approaches to assess the environmental impact of space missions, both in orbit and on Earth, and to create guidelines that help reduce the harms of future missions. The “Look Up!” project focused on public engagement as a key part of this effort, supporting the UK’s commitment to responsible space activity and the preservation of the night sky.

Two public engagement activities were created. A mobile exhibit, developed with the Jodrell Bank Centre for Engagement, used a large interactive LED display to show real-time satellite passes over the viewer’s location. An open digital codebase and set of classroom resources, co-created with teachers, allowed students to generate their own visualisations and explore the impact of satellites on astronomy and the night sky. Both activities aimed to make the scale of orbital activity visible and personal, encouraging wider public awareness before lasting changes to the night sky become irreversible.
